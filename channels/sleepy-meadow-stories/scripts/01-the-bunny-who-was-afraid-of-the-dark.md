# The Bunny Who Was Afraid of the Dark
Channel: Sleepy Meadow Stories (original characters — no filming, fully automated)
Format: Narrated illustrated bedtime story | Target length: ~5 min | Audience: toddlers/preschoolers (2-5) + parents

---

**[0:00–0:15] COLD OPEN / HOOK**
(VO, soft, warm, slow pace — true "bedtime" cadence)

In a meadow where the fireflies live, there was a little bunny named Pip. Pip loved hopping in the sunshine. But when the sun went down... Pip did not love the dark. Not even a little bit.

**[0:15–1:00] SETUP**

Every night, Pip's burrow filled up with shadows. The coat hook looked like a long-armed shadow-giant. The curtain looked like it was breathing. Pip would pull the blanket all the way up to his nose and whisper, "Mama? Is it safe?"

And every night, Mama Rabbit would say the same thing: "The dark isn't empty, Pip. It's just resting. Just like you."

**[1:00–2:00] THE PROBLEM GROWS**

One night, the wind knocked a branch against the window — tap, tap, tap — and Pip jumped so high he bonked his head on the top of the burrow! He ran to find Mama, but Mama was out gathering clover for breakfast. Pip was going to have to be brave all by himself.

He sat very still. The tapping came again. Tap. Tap. Pip's heart went thump-thump-thump, fast as a rabbit can run.

**[2:00–3:30] THE TURN**

Then Pip remembered something Mama always did — she'd open the curtain, just a little, "to let the dark introduce itself." Pip's paws were shaking, but he hopped to the window and nudged the curtain open.

And there... was the branch. Just a branch. Swaying in the breeze, same as always, with a little owl friend named Oona perched right on top of it, blinking her big round eyes.

"Oh!" said Pip. "It's just you, branch. And hello, Oona."

Oona hooted softly, which in owl means "Good evening, small friend." Pip almost laughed. The tapping wasn't scary at all once he could see what it was.

**[3:30–4:30] RESOLUTION**

Pip looked up at the sky. The stars were out, blinking on one by one like the meadow's own nightlights. The moon sat round and gentle above the trees, the same moon that watched over every burrow in the meadow — his, and his friends', and probably yours too.

"The dark isn't empty," Pip whispered, remembering Mama's words. "It's just resting."

When Mama came home with her basket of clover, she found Pip already curled up, curtain open just a crack, fast asleep with the smallest, bravest smile on his face.

**[4:30–5:00] GOODNIGHT CLOSE / CTA**
(VO slows further, almost a lullaby cadence)

And if you ever feel like Pip tonight — if the shadows look a little too long, or the house makes a sound you don't know yet — remember what he learned: open the curtain just a little, and let the dark introduce itself.

Goodnight, little one. Sleep soft, sleep safe. We'll see you in the meadow again soon.
