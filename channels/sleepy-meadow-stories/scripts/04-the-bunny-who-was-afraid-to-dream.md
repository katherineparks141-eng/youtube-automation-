# The Bunny Who Was Afraid to Dream
Channel: Sleepy Meadow Stories (original characters — no filming, fully automated)
Format: Narrated illustrated bedtime story | Target length: ~5 min | Audience: toddlers/preschoolers (2-5) + parents
Pillar: Pip the Bunny (4th story — continues from "The Bunny Who Was Afraid of the Dark,"
"The Bunny Who Counted the Stars," and "The Bunny Who Didn't Want to Miss Anything")

---

**[0:00–0:15] COLD OPEN / HOOK**
(VO, soft, warm, slow pace — true "bedtime" cadence)

Pip the bunny sat up very straight in his bed, both paws pressed flat against the
blanket. "I don't want to close my eyes," he whispered to the quiet room. "Not tonight."

**[0:15–1:00] SETUP**

Mama Rabbit came and sat beside him. "What's wrong, little one?" Pip's ears drooped.
"Last night I had a dream about a big shadow, and it chased me, and it felt so real." He
pulled his blanket up to his chin. "What if it comes back? What if I close my eyes and
it's waiting for me?" He didn't want to be scared of his own bed, but he was.

**[1:00–2:00] THE PROBLEM GROWS**

Pip tried everything he could think of to stay a little bit awake, just in case. He kept
one eye open at a time, like a lookout. He hummed very quietly so his ears would have
something to listen to besides the dark. But staying half-awake all night sounded even
more tiring than any shadow dream, and his eyes kept drooping no matter how hard he tried
to guard them.

**[2:00–3:30] THE TURN**

Oona landed on the windowsill with a soft rustle. "I heard someone was having trouble
with their dreams," she said gently. Pip nodded, miserable. Oona tilted her head. "Did
you know, Pip — you get to choose who comes into your dream with you?" Pip blinked. "I
do?" "Mm-hmm," said Oona. "Before I sleep, I always picture someone who makes me feel
safe, standing right beside me. Then wherever the dream goes, I'm not alone there." Mama
Rabbit smiled. "Who would you want standing next to you, Pip?" Pip thought for a long
moment. "You," he said. "And Oona. And maybe my favorite blanket, holding my paw." "Then
that's who comes," said Oona. "Picture it now, nice and clear, before your eyes get
heavy — your three guardians, right there with you. A shadow doesn't feel nearly so big
when you're not facing it alone."

**[3:30–4:30] RESOLUTION**

Pip closed his eyes and pictured it exactly like Oona said: Mama Rabbit on one side, Oona
perched on the other, his blanket wrapped snug around his paw. He held the picture
steady, the way you'd hold a lantern. If the shadow came back tonight, he wouldn't be the
only one there to meet it. His shoulders loosened. His ears stopped straining to listen
for trouble. And somewhere between one slow breath and the next, Pip drifted off still
holding his little picture of the three of them together — and this time, when a dream
did come, it came with company, and it didn't feel nearly as big as before.

**[4:30–5:00] GOODNIGHT CLOSE / CTA**
(VO slows further, almost a lullaby cadence)

So if you're feeling a little scared of what your dreams might bring tonight, try what
Pip tried: picture the people who make you feel safe, standing right there beside you.
You don't have to meet the dark alone — and you never really were.

Goodnight, little one. Sleep soft, sleep safe. We'll see you in the meadow again soon.

[End screen: soft chime, link to the next Sleepy Meadow story]
