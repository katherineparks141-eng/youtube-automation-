# The Bunny Who Didn't Want to Miss Anything
Channel: Sleepy Meadow Stories (original characters — no filming, fully automated)
Format: Narrated illustrated bedtime story | Target length: ~5 min | Audience: toddlers/preschoolers (2-5) + parents
Pillar: Pip the Bunny (3rd story — continues from "The Bunny Who Was Afraid of the Dark" and
"The Bunny Who Counted the Stars")

---

**[0:00–0:15] COLD OPEN / HOOK**
(VO, soft, warm, slow pace — true "bedtime" cadence)

Pip the bunny stood at his bedroom door with one paw still on the handle, not quite ready
to go in. "But what if something fun happens," he said, "and I'm asleep, and I miss it?"

**[0:15–1:00] SETUP**

Mama Rabbit knelt down beside him. "Like what, little one?" Pip thought hard. "What if the
fireflies have a party? What if a new flower blooms? What if Oona tells a really good
joke and I'm not there to hear it?" He said this so fast his ears wiggled. Bedtime, to
Pip, felt like a door closing on all the good parts of being awake.

**[1:00–2:00] THE PROBLEM GROWS**

That night Pip tried to stay awake on purpose. He propped his eyes open with his paws. He
counted his own whiskers. He even tried humming to keep himself alert. But the harder he
tried to catch every last bit of the day, the more tired and grumpy and *not* fun he felt
— which was strange, because staying awake was supposed to be the fun part.

**[2:00–3:30] THE TURN**

Oona landed softly on the windowsill, the way she always did when a small rabbit needed
her. "You know," she said, "the meadow doesn't stop being wonderful just because your
eyes are closed. It waits for you. The fireflies will still blink tomorrow. The flower
will still be new in the morning — new things don't rush off anywhere." Pip frowned.
"But what if I miss the best part?" Oona tilted her head. "Sleep *is* a best part, Pip.
It's where today gets tucked away safe, so tomorrow has room to start fresh. And
sometimes," she added, with a soft hoot of a laugh, "the best dreams are better than any
party."

**[3:30–4:30] RESOLUTION**

Pip thought about that. He thought about the acorn he'd found, and the puddle he'd
jumped, and the funny wobble in Oona's hoot. None of those good things had happened while
he was trying to stay awake and catch more — they'd already happened, and they were his
to keep, tucked away safe just like Oona said. Pip let his eyes close, just to test it.
Behind his eyelids, the meadow was still there, soft and glowing, waiting kindly instead
of racing off without him. "Oh," Pip whispered, surprised. "It waited." And somewhere
between one slow breath and the next, Pip's dream picked him up gently and carried him
off to a meadow even softer than the real one.

**[4:30–5:00] GOODNIGHT CLOSE / CTA**
(VO slows further, almost a lullaby cadence)

So if you're holding your eyes open tonight, worried you'll miss something good — don't
worry, little one. The world is patient. It will still be there in the morning, exactly
as wonderful as you left it. And whatever waits behind your own closed eyes tonight might
just be the best part of all.

Goodnight, little one. Sleep soft, sleep safe. We'll see you in the meadow again soon.

[End screen: soft chime, link to the next Sleepy Meadow story]
