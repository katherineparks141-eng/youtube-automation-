# The Bunny Who Was Afraid to Say Sorry
Channel: Sleepy Meadow Stories (original characters — no filming, fully automated)
Format: Narrated illustrated bedtime story | Target length: ~5 min | Audience: toddlers/preschoolers (2-5) + parents
Pillar: Pip the Bunny (8th story — continues from "The Bunny Who Was Afraid of the Dark,"
"The Bunny Who Counted the Stars," "The Bunny Who Didn't Want to Miss Anything," "The
Bunny Who Was Afraid to Dream," "The Bunny Who Worried About Tomorrow," "The Bunny Who
Was Scared of the Storm," and "The Bunny Who Was Afraid to Try")

Theme note: distinct from stories 1-7 — this one is about the heavy, guilty feeling
after accidentally hurting a friend's feelings, and being afraid to say sorry because
it might make things worse, not fear of the dark, FOMO, nightmares, future-worry, a
loud noise, or fear of trying a new skill. The fix here is Oona reframing "sorry" as a
small brave gift you give, not a confession that makes the hurt bigger — and a simple,
practiced first line to say, not a sound-counting trick, a pouch, a cloud, picturing
guardians, or a "first small try" framing.

---

**[0:00–0:15] COLD OPEN / HOOK**
(VO, soft, warm, slow pace — true "bedtime" cadence)

That afternoon, Pip had knocked over Fern the field mouse's tower of acorn-cap cups —
by accident, mid-game — and instead of helping rebuild it, he'd scampered off
embarrassed. All evening, a small heavy feeling sat in his chest, right under his
ribs, and wouldn't go away.

*Visual: Quick daytime flashback moment — Pip frozen mid-step, ears flat, looking back
at a toppled little tower of acorn-cap cups while Fern (a small soft-gray field mouse,
simple and friendly-styled) looks down at the scattered cups, surprised rather than
upset. Soft daylight palette, gentle and not dramatic.*

**[0:15–1:00] SETUP**

Back in his nest that night, Pip couldn't settle. He kept picturing Fern's face when
the tower fell. "I should say sorry," he murmured to his blanket, "but what if she's
still mad? What if saying it just makes her remember how bad it was, and makes
everything worse?" So he decided the safer thing was to say nothing at all, and just
hope the heavy feeling went away on its own by morning.

*Visual: Pip curled tight in his willow-hollow nest, blanket pulled up to his chin,
staring at the ceiling with worried eyes. Soft rose/lavender twilight palette,
consistent with the established nest setting.*

**[1:00–2:00] THE PROBLEM GROWS**

But the heavy feeling didn't go anywhere. It sat there while he tried to count
fireflies. It sat there while he tried to think about tomorrow's breakfast. Every time
he closed his eyes, he saw the tower falling again, and Fern's startled little face.
"Maybe if I just play with someone else tomorrow," he whispered, "I won't have to
think about it." But even saying that made the heavy feeling sit heavier, like a
stone he was trying to carry around instead of just putting down.

*Visual: Pip tossing softly in his nest, a small dim thought-bubble above him replaying
the toppled tower. Keep it gentle and non-alarming — a soft gray cloud-shape, not a
scary image. His ears droop lower each time it repeats.*

**[2:00–3:30] THE TURN**

Oona the owl landed on the branch outside, tilting her head at Pip's restless
shuffling. "That's not your usual wiggling, little one. What's sitting on your
chest tonight?" Pip told her everything — the tower, Fern's face, the scampering
off, the heavy feeling that wouldn't leave. Oona made a soft, understanding
hoot. "Ah. You're not afraid of Fern being mad, Pip. You're afraid that saying
sorry means the bad moment gets bigger. But it's the opposite — a sorry is a
small, brave gift. It doesn't make the bump worse. It's how you help carry it
away, together." Pip's ears lifted just slightly. "But what if I don't know
what to say?" "Then we practice," Oona said warmly. "Just four small words to
start: 'I knocked it down.' The rest follows on its own once you begin."

*Visual: Oona perched just inside the hollow opening, wing resting gently near
Pip as she speaks, his tightly curled posture slowly loosening. Warm, cozy
nighttime palette — soft mushroom-glow gold and twilight indigo, consistent
with the channel's established visual language.*

**[3:30–4:30] RESOLUTION**

The next morning, Pip found Fern restacking her acorn-cap cups by the old
mossy log. His paws felt fluttery, but he remembered Oona's four words. "Fern —
I knocked it down," he said, "and I ran off instead of helping. I'm sorry. Can
I help you build it again?" Fern looked up, surprised, then smiled — a real,
easy smile. "I was more sad you ran off than about the cups," she said. "Thank
you for coming back." Together they rebuilt the tower, taller than before, and
Pip noticed the heavy feeling in his chest had quietly lifted away, like it had
never really needed to stay so long. That night, tucked into his nest, he told
Mama Rabbit all about it. "I was so scared sorry would make it worse," he said,
already yawning. "And instead?" Mama Rabbit asked, smiling. "Instead it just...
put it down," Pip murmured. "The stone's gone."

*Visual: Daytime scene by a mossy log — Pip and Fern rebuilding the acorn-cap
tower together, both smiling, taller stack than before. Then a soft transition
back to nighttime: Pip back in his nest, light and peaceful, Mama Rabbit beside
him.*

**[4:30–5:00] GOODNIGHT CLOSE / CTA**
(VO slows further, almost a lullaby cadence)

So if you ever do something that leaves a heavy feeling in your chest, remember
what Pip learned: saying sorry doesn't make the bump bigger. It's how you put
the stone down together. Just four small words to start, and the rest follows
on its own.

Goodnight, little one. Sleep soft, sleep safe. We'll see you in the meadow
again soon.

[End screen: soft chime, link to the next Sleepy Meadow story]
