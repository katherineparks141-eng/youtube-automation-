# The Bunny Who Worried About Tomorrow
Channel: Sleepy Meadow Stories (original characters — no filming, fully automated)
Format: Narrated illustrated bedtime story | Target length: ~5 min | Audience: toddlers/preschoolers (2-5) + parents
Pillar: Pip the Bunny (5th story — continues from "The Bunny Who Was Afraid of the Dark,"
"The Bunny Who Counted the Stars," "The Bunny Who Didn't Want to Miss Anything," and
"The Bunny Who Was Afraid to Dream")

Theme note: distinct from stories 1-4 — this one is about anxious anticipation of a
*specific future event* (tomorrow's meadow sing-along), not fear of dark, racing
bedtime thoughts in general, FOMO about missing fun tonight, or nightmares.

---

**[0:00–0:15] COLD OPEN / HOOK**
(VO, soft, warm, slow pace — true "bedtime" cadence)

Pip the bunny lay very still under his blanket, eyes wide open, staring at the ceiling of
his willow-hollow nest. "Tomorrow," he whispered, "is a very big day." And the thought of
it was sitting right on his chest like a small, heavy stone.

*Visual: Pip lying in his thistledown nest inside the willow hollow, eyes open, twilight
palette, one paw on his chest. Calm, not distressed — this is quiet worry, not panic.*

**[0:15–1:00] SETUP**

Tomorrow was the meadow's spring sing-along, and this year — for the very first time —
Pip had a line all his own to sing, right in front of everyone. Mama Rabbit came to tuck
him in and found him wide awake. "What's keeping those eyes open, little one?" Pip's
ears drooped low. "What if I forget my words? What if my voice wobbles? What if everyone
is looking right at me and I mess it up?" He pulled the blanket up over his nose. "I keep
thinking about it and thinking about it and it won't stop."

*Visual: Mama Rabbit sitting beside the nest, one paw resting near Pip's. Soft worry on
Pip's face — ears low, not wide-eyed fear. Warm lavender/rose palette.*

**[1:00–2:00] THE PROBLEM GROWS**

Pip tried to push the worry away, but it just kept tapping him on the shoulder. He tried
humming his line to practice it — and got the words backward. He tried picturing the
sing-along — and in his mind, he dropped his line and everyone gasped. The more he
thought about tomorrow, the bigger and heavier tomorrow felt, until it was all he could
think about at all, and sleep felt impossibly far away.

*Visual: Soft, non-scary visual motif — a small round "worry cloud" shape (gentle,
rounded, gray-lavender, never sharp or menacing) floating just above Pip's head, growing
slightly larger each time his thoughts circle back to tomorrow. Keep it whimsical and
soft, like a thought-bubble, not frightening.*

**[2:00–3:30] THE TURN**

That's when Oona the owl landed softly on the branch outside, feathers rustling. "I heard
a lot of thinking happening in here," she said kindly. "Tell me — is the worry about
tonight, or about tomorrow?" Pip blinked. "...Tomorrow." Oona nodded slowly. "Then here's
a secret: tonight's job isn't to solve tomorrow. Tonight's only job is to rest, so
tomorrow-you has the energy to handle tomorrow's things." Pip frowned. "But what if I
don't stop thinking about it?" "Then we give the worry somewhere to wait," said Oona.
"Picture a little pouch, right by your nest. Every time the worry taps your shoulder
tonight, you don't have to carry it — you just set it gently in the pouch, and tell it,
'you can wait for morning.' It'll still be there if you need it. But you don't have to
hold it all night long."

*Visual: Oona perched on the willow branch, speaking to Pip. A small woven pouch (simple,
soft-colored, no basket-weave resembling a wicker Peter-Rabbit-style basket — keep it a
flat little drawstring pouch, mushroom-gold thread) appears beside Pip's nest, glowing
faintly. The worry cloud shape drifts toward it.*

**[3:30–4:30] RESOLUTION**

Pip tried it. The next time the worry tapped his shoulder — *what if I forget my
words?* — he pictured setting it gently into the little pouch. "You can wait for
morning," he murmured. The worry cloud floated down, smaller now, and settled inside.
Another worry tapped — *what if my voice wobbles?* — and he set that one in too, until
the pouch sat quiet and closed beside his nest, and his chest didn't feel so heavy
anymore. "They're not gone," Mama Rabbit said softly, smoothing his blanket, "but they
don't have to live in your head tonight. Morning-you can open the pouch whenever you're
ready." Pip's eyes grew heavy at last. His line would still be there tomorrow — but so
would Mama Rabbit, and Oona, and the whole meadow cheering for him, not against him. And
somewhere between one slow breath and the next, Pip drifted off to sleep, the little
pouch resting quiet and still beside him.

*Visual: Pip's eyes closing, the worry-cloud shapes inside the pouch now small, faint,
and still (not gone, just resting). Final wide shot: Pip asleep in his nest, the closed
pouch glowing faintly beside him, fireflies and glowing mushrooms lighting the hollow,
Mama Rabbit's silhouette settling nearby.*

**[4:30–5:00] GOODNIGHT CLOSE / CTA**
(VO slows further, almost a lullaby cadence)

So if a worry about tomorrow is sitting heavy on your chest tonight, try what Pip tried:
you don't have to solve it right now, and you don't have to carry it all night either.
Just set it down gently, and tell it — it can wait for morning.

Goodnight, little one. Sleep soft, sleep safe. We'll see you in the meadow again soon.

[End screen: soft chime, link to the next Sleepy Meadow story]
