# The Bunny Who Was Scared of the Storm
Channel: Sleepy Meadow Stories (original characters — no filming, fully automated)
Format: Narrated illustrated bedtime story | Target length: ~5 min | Audience: toddlers/preschoolers (2-5) + parents
Pillar: Pip the Bunny (6th story — continues from "The Bunny Who Was Afraid of the Dark,"
"The Bunny Who Counted the Stars," "The Bunny Who Didn't Want to Miss Anything," "The
Bunny Who Was Afraid to Dream," and "The Bunny Who Worried About Tomorrow")

Theme note: distinct from stories 1-5 — this one is about a loud, sudden *outside* scare
(thunder) in the moment it happens, not fear of the dark in general, racing bedtime
thoughts, FOMO, nightmares, or worry about a future event. The fix here is a sound-based
grounding trick (turning a scary noise into a countable, predictable rhythm), not a
pouch, a cloud, or picturing guardians.

---

**[0:00–0:15] COLD OPEN / HOOK**
(VO, soft, warm, slow pace — true "bedtime" cadence)

Far off over the meadow, the sky gave a low, rolling grumble. Pip's ears shot straight
up. "W-what was that?" he whispered, pulling his blanket all the way to his chin.

*Visual: Pip sitting bolt upright in his thistledown nest inside the willow hollow, ears
up and alert, blanket clutched to his chin. Twilight palette, a few distant soft gray
clouds visible through the hollow's leafy opening — no lightning yet, keep it gentle.*

**[0:15–1:00] SETUP**

A storm was rolling in over Sleepy Meadow — the kind that came most springs, with big
soft raindrops and a grumbly sky. Mama Rabbit came to check on him just as a second
rumble rolled through, longer this time. Pip burrowed down into his nest. "The sky is
growling, Mama. Is it angry?" Mama Rabbit settled beside him, her ears calm and low.
"It's not angry, little one. It's just loud. But I know loud can still feel scary, even
when it isn't dangerous."

*Visual: Mama Rabbit sitting close beside Pip's nest, one paw near his, both ears low
and relaxed to model calm. Soft rose/lavender palette, a little more cloud cover drifting
past the hollow opening outside.*

**[1:00–2:00] THE PROBLEM GROWS**

The rumbles kept coming, closer together now, each one making Pip flinch and burrow
deeper under his blanket. He tried covering his ears, but he could still feel the sound
in his chest. He tried to guess when the next one would come, but every time he thought
it was over, another low rumble rolled through, and his heart went *thump-thump-thump*
right along with it.

*Visual: Pip curled into a tight ball under his blanket, only his eyes peeking out, ears
pressed flat against his head each time thunder sounds. Keep the storm itself gentle and
non-threatening — soft gray clouds, rounded raindrops, no jagged lightning bolts, no
dark scary colors — the SCARE is in Pip's reaction, not in making the storm look
frightening to young viewers.*

**[2:00–3:30] THE TURN**

Oona the owl glided in out of the drizzle and shook the rain from her feathers on the
branch outside. "Still awake in there?" she asked. Pip nodded, ears flat. "The sky keeps
growling and I don't know when it'll stop." Oona tilted her head. "May I tell you my
favorite storm trick? When the sky flashes bright, that's the lightning saying hello.
Then we count, slow and steady — one meadow-mouse, two meadow-mouse, three meadow-mouse
— until the rumble answers back. That's just the thunder saying hello right after,
taking its time to catch up. It's not two different scary things. It's one big
conversation, and once you're counting, you're not just waiting and wondering anymore —
you know exactly what's coming next." Pip blinked. "So... it's not random?" "Not one
bit," said Oona. "Try it with me."

*Visual: Oona perched just inside the hollow opening, rain beaded on her feathers, one
wing gesturing gently as she "counts." A soft flash of pale gold light outside (lightning
kept soft and brief, more like a firefly-bright glow than a sharp bolt), then Pip's ears
perking with curiosity instead of fear as he starts counting on his paws.*

**[3:30–4:30] RESOLUTION**

The next soft flash came, and Pip counted along: "One meadow-mouse, two meadow-mouse,
three meadow-mouse—" and sure enough, the rumble answered, right on cue, just like Oona
said it would. He counted again on the next one, and the one after that, and each time
the rumble arrived exactly when he expected it, the thump-thump-thump in his chest grew
quieter. "It's not so scary when I know it's coming," he said, voice slowing with a yawn.
"That's it exactly," Mama Rabbit murmured, tucking the blanket snug around him. Outside,
the rain settled into a soft, steady patter against the willow leaves — no longer a
growl to fear, just the meadow's own gentle lullaby. Pip's counting slowed, his eyes grew
heavy, and somewhere between one soft rumble and the next, he drifted off to sleep.

*Visual: Pip's counting paws slowing, eyes drooping. Final wide shot: Pip asleep in his
nest, rain pattering softly outside the willow hollow (gentle, rhythmic, not harsh),
Mama Rabbit's silhouette settled nearby, Oona still perched on the branch, glowing
mushrooms and fireflies lighting the hollow despite the rain.*

**[4:30–5:00] GOODNIGHT CLOSE / CTA**
(VO slows further, almost a lullaby cadence)

So if a storm — or any loud, sudden sound — ever makes your heart go thump-thump-thump
tonight, try what Pip tried: count slow and steady between the flash and the rumble.
Once you know what's coming next, loud doesn't have to feel so scary.

Goodnight, little one. Sleep soft, sleep safe. We'll see you in the meadow again soon.
