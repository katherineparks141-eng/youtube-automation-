# The Bunny Who Was Afraid to Ask for Help
Channel: Sleepy Meadow Stories (original characters — no filming, fully automated)
Format: Narrated illustrated bedtime story | Target length: ~5 min | Audience: toddlers/preschoolers (2-5) + parents
Pillar: Pip the Bunny (9th story — continues from "The Bunny Who Was Afraid of the Dark,"
"The Bunny Who Counted the Stars," "The Bunny Who Didn't Want to Miss Anything," "The
Bunny Who Was Afraid to Dream," "The Bunny Who Worried About Tomorrow," "The Bunny Who
Was Scared of the Storm," "The Bunny Who Was Afraid to Try," and "The Bunny Who Was
Afraid to Say Sorry")

Theme note: distinct from stories 1-8 — this one is about Pip getting stuck (literally,
his scarf snagged on a bramble) and feeling too embarrassed to call for help because he
thinks needing help means he's not big enough yet, not fear of the dark, racing
thoughts, FOMO, nightmares, future-worry, a loud noise, fear of trying something new, or
guilt over hurting a friend. The fix here is Oona reframing asking for help as something
even the biggest, wisest creatures in the meadow do, not a sign of being small — and a
simple practiced call to use, not four words of apology, a reframe about wobbling, or a
stone-carrying metaphor (those belong to other stories).

---

**[0:00–0:15] COLD OPEN / HOOK**
(VO, soft, warm, slow pace — true "bedtime" cadence)

That evening, on the way home from the meadow, Pip's scarf caught tight on a bramble
bush at the edge of the path — and the harder he wriggled, the more stuck he got.

*Visual: Pip in the blue-dusk meadow path, his mustard scarf snagged on a low bramble
bush, leaning away from it with wide surprised eyes. Keep the bramble small and soft-
edged, not thorny or scary-looking — a gentle visual obstacle, not a hazard.*

**[0:15–1:00] SETUP**

He could have called out for Mama Rabbit — her burrow was just past the old oak — but
something in his chest made him hesitate. "If I call for help over a silly scarf," he
thought, "everyone will think I'm still too little to manage anything on my own." So
instead of calling out, he decided to wriggle free all by himself, quietly, before
anyone noticed.

*Visual: Pip glancing toward a warm, distant glow of light (Mama Rabbit's burrow window)
through the trees, then looking down and tugging at his scarf alone, jaw set in quiet
determination. Soft twilight palette.*

**[1:00–2:00] THE PROBLEM GROWS**

He tugged this way. He tugged that way. He tried spinning in a slow circle, which only
wound the scarf tighter. The evening grew cooler and the fireflies began to blink on
around him, and still he was stuck — more stuck, if anything, than when he'd started.
"Maybe if I just stay very still," he whispered to himself, "no one will even know I
needed help at all." But staying still didn't loosen the scarf, and the cool evening air
made him shiver, just a little.

*Visual: Pip increasingly tangled, scarf wound a bit tighter around the bramble, fireflies
beginning to glow around him in the darkening meadow. His ears droop with frustration,
not fear — keep this gentle and mildly comic, not distressing.*

**[2:00–3:30] THE TURN**

Oona the owl, gliding home along her usual evening path, spotted the little shape
struggling by the bramble and landed softly on a low branch nearby. "Stuck fast, are
we?" she asked kindly. Pip's ears went hot with embarrassment. "I didn't want to call
for help," he admitted. "I thought it would mean I'm not big enough to manage things
myself." Oona gave a low, warm hoot of a laugh. "Pip, I am the oldest, wisest owl in this
whole meadow, and even I call for help — when the wind's too strong to fly home alone, I
call to the other owls, and they come. Asking for help isn't the size of you shrinking.
It's the size of your meadow growing, because now you have more hands — or wings — to
hold things with." Pip's ears lifted, just slightly. "But what do I even say?" "Just
this," said Oona. "'I'm stuck — will you help me?' Plain and simple. The bramble doesn't
care how brave you sound. It only cares that someone comes."

*Visual: Oona perched on a low branch at Pip's eye level, head tilted warmly, one wing
gesturing toward the distant glow of Mama Rabbit's window. Pip's posture softening from
tense to open. Warm mushroom-glow gold mixed with the cooling twilight indigo.*

**[3:30–4:30] RESOLUTION**

Pip took a breath and called out, a little wobbly at first: "I'm stuck — will you help
me?" It wasn't even a moment before Mama Rabbit came hurrying through the ferns, and
together — her gentle paws, his careful wriggling — the scarf came free in no time at
all. "I was worried you'd think I was too little," Pip confessed on the walk home. Mama
Rabbit nuzzled the top of his head. "Asking for help is one of the bravest, biggest
things a bunny can do," she said. "It's never the small thing to do — it's the wise
thing." Tucked into his nest that night, warm and finally unstuck, Pip murmured, already
half-asleep, "Next time I get stuck... I'll just ask." "That's all it takes," Mama
Rabbit whispered, tucking the (now unsnagged) scarf around him like an extra blanket.

*Visual: Mama Rabbit and Pip together freeing the scarf from the bramble in the near-dark
meadow, then a soft transition to Pip's willow-hollow nest, Mama Rabbit tucking the
scarf around him, both peaceful and warm.*

**[4:30–5:00] GOODNIGHT CLOSE / CTA**
(VO slows further, almost a lullaby cadence)

So if you ever feel stuck on something, and a small voice tells you that asking for help
means you're not big enough — remember what Pip learned: asking for help is never the
small thing to do. It's the wise thing, and the bravest thing, too.

Goodnight, little one. Sleep soft, sleep safe. We'll see you in the meadow again soon.
