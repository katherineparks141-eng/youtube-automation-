# The Bunny Who Was Afraid to Try
Channel: Sleepy Meadow Stories (original characters — no filming, fully automated)
Format: Narrated illustrated bedtime story | Target length: ~5 min | Audience: toddlers/preschoolers (2-5) + parents
Pillar: Pip the Bunny (7th story — continues from "The Bunny Who Was Afraid of the Dark,"
"The Bunny Who Counted the Stars," "The Bunny Who Didn't Want to Miss Anything," "The
Bunny Who Was Afraid to Dream," "The Bunny Who Worried About Tomorrow," and "The Bunny
Who Was Scared of the Storm")

Theme note: distinct from stories 1-6 — this one is about fear of *trying* a new skill
in front of others and risking getting it wrong, not fear of the dark, FOMO, nightmares,
future-worry, or a sudden loud noise. The fix here is a "wobble is part of learning"
reframe plus breaking the scary big thing into small counted tries, not a sound-counting
trick, a pouch, a cloud, or picturing guardians.

---

**[0:00–0:15] COLD OPEN / HOOK**
(VO, soft, warm, slow pace — true "bedtime" cadence)

Down by the lily-pad stream, three young rabbits were learning to hop stone to stone.
Pip watched from the grass, paws tucked under his chin. "What if I hop and I fall in?"
he whispered to no one in particular.

*Visual: Pip crouched in tall grass at the stream's edge, watching two other young
rabbits (simple, soft-style silhouettes, not detailed named characters) hopping stone to
stone across the lily-pad stream. Twilight-leaning daylight palette consistent with the
channel's soft painterly style, water gently rippling.*

**[0:15–1:00] SETUP**

That evening, Pip's whole body still felt jittery thinking about it. Mama Rabbit found
him curled in his nest, chewing his lip. "You've been quiet since the stream," she said,
settling beside him. "I want to hop the stones too, Mama. But what if I try and everyone
sees me wobble and fall?" Mama Rabbit's ears stayed low and gentle. "Oh, little one. Of
course that thought feels big. But wobbling isn't the same as failing — wobbling is just
what trying looks like, right before it turns into knowing how."

*Visual: Mama Rabbit sitting close beside Pip's nest in the willow hollow, one paw
resting near his, both ears low and calm. Soft rose/lavender twilight palette, consistent
with the channel's established nest setting.*

**[1:00–2:00] THE PROBLEM GROWS**

Pip squeezed his eyes shut and pictured it anyway: his paw slipping, a splash, the other
rabbits laughing. The more he pictured it, the more his ears drooped and his tummy
tightened. "Maybe I just won't ever try," he mumbled into his blanket. "Then I can't get
it wrong." But even saying that out loud made him feel smaller, not better — like he was
hiding from the stream instead of just staying home by choice.

*Visual: Pip burrowed low in his nest, blanket pulled up, eyes squeezed shut, picturing a
small worried thought-bubble of himself slipping off a lily-pad stone. Keep the imagined
"fall" gentle and cartoon-soft, not alarming — a tiny splash, not a scary plunge.*

**[2:00–3:30] THE TURN**

Oona the owl landed softly on the branch outside, shaking a little dew from her wings.
"Why the long ears tonight?" she asked. Pip explained about the stream, the stones, the
wobble he was so sure was coming. Oona tilted her head thoughtfully. "May I tell you a
secret about every single creature who ever learned to hop those stones — your mama
included? Every one of them wobbled first. Not might have. Did. The wobble isn't the
part before you learn — the wobble IS part of learning." Pip blinked. "So the big rabbits
wobbled too?" "Every one," Oona said. "Tomorrow, you don't have to hop all five stones.
Just try the very first one. One small try. That's the whole job."

*Visual: Oona perched just inside the hollow opening, one wing gesturing warmly as she
speaks, Pip's ears slowly lifting from drooped to curious. A soft, cozy nighttime palette
— no stream visible here, just the two of them talking in the glowing hollow.*

**[3:30–4:30] RESOLUTION**

The next afternoon, Pip stood at the stream's edge again — this time his paws still felt
fluttery, but he remembered Oona's words. Not all five stones. Just the first one. He
hopped — and wobbled, exactly like Oona said he might — caught his balance with a little
flap of his ears, and laughed instead of panicking. "I wobbled! And I'm still dry!" The
other rabbits cheered, not at a perfect hop, but at a brave one. Pip tried the second
stone, wobbled less. By the fourth stone, his hops felt almost steady. That night, tucked
back in his nest, he told Mama Rabbit all about it. "I didn't do it perfectly," he said,
already yawning. "That's exactly right," Mama Rabbit murmured, tucking the blanket snug
around him. "Perfectly was never the job. Trying was."

*Visual: Daytime stream scene — Pip mid-hop on the first stone, one paw wobbling for
balance, a small splash of water droplets (gentle, not alarming), the other young rabbits
nearby with warm, encouraging expressions rather than laughing-at poses. Then a soft
transition back to nighttime: Pip back in his nest, sleepy and pleased with himself.*

**[4:30–5:00] GOODNIGHT CLOSE / CTA**
(VO slows further, almost a lullaby cadence)

So if there's something new you'd like to try tomorrow, but the wobble-thought creeps in
first — remember what Pip learned: you don't have to do it perfectly. You just have to
try the first small step. Wobbling means you're learning, not failing.

Goodnight, little one. Sleep soft, sleep safe. We'll see you in the meadow again soon.
