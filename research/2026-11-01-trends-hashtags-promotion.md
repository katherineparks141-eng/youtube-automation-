# Trends, hashtags, and promotion — research notes (Nov 1, 2026)

Backing research for "Packaging rules" and "Promotion" in `CLAUDE.md`.

## What's working right now in faceless/AI-narrated channels

- The AI-narrated "faceless channel" niche (history/folklore, Reddit-story, true-crime,
  bedtime-story formats) has grown fast specifically because narration + stills is cheap
  to produce and YouTube does not penalize it for being AI-made *as long as it's
  transparently disclosed and isn't spam-reposted/templated content*. YouTube's
  synthetic-media policy change (effective across 2026) requires disclosure for
  realistic AI content — this is why the AI-disclosure line is a hard rule, not just a
  courtesy.
- Channels in this niche that plateau early are almost always the ones that look
  identical video-to-video (same thumbnail template, same stock-photo-feeling visuals,
  same cold open line). The ones that keep growing vary their hook, their visual pacing,
  and their packaging per video even within a consistent brand identity — this directly
  informs the "avoid anything that reads as mass-templated" promotion rule.
- History/folklore specifically rewards *regional or lesser-known* stories over
  already-oversaturated ones (Bell Witch is a reasonable starting pick precisely because
  it's well-documented enough to cite sources, but isn't as oversaturated as e.g.
  Amityville).

## Hashtags & captions

- YouTube's own guidance: hashtags above the first ~3-5 shown are hidden/truncated in
  display, and a video with **more than 15 hashtags total has all of them ignored** by
  the system (treated as spam). Practical target: 3–5 in the description, with the most
  important one inside the first 100 characters (it also appears above the title).
- Reusing an identical hashtag set on every single video reads as templated/spam to
  both viewers and, per some creator reporting, to the recommendation system itself.
  Keep 1–2 branded hashtags constant for channel identity (e.g. `#ForgottenAndFound`,
  `#SleepyMeadowStories`) and rotate the rest per topic.
- Auto-captions should stay on for every video — most watch time on both history and
  kids content happens on mobile with sound sometimes off (parents playing content for
  kids in the background, commuters), and captions measurably help average view
  duration and accessibility.

## Promotion tactics

- Thumbnail A/B testing (see cadence note) is the single highest-leverage free
  promotion lever available post-upload.
- Playlists by content pillar/theme compound session watch time once a channel has 3+
  videos in the same pillar — YouTube will chain-recommend within a playlist much more
  reliably than it will across unrelated single videos.
- Traffic-source breakdown (Studio → Reach tab) should be checked per video to see
  whether Browse/Suggested or Search is doing the work — history/folklore content
  tends to get meaningful long-tail Search traffic (people searching the story name
  itself), while bedtime-story content leans more on Browse/Suggested and session
  watching from parents queuing up multiple videos at once. Packaging (titles especially)
  should be tuned differently per channel based on this.

## Kids-channel-specific notes (Sleepy Meadow)

- Calm, accurate thumbnails outperform exaggerated ones for children's content (see
  cadence note) — this is a reversal of general-audience best practice and is called
  out explicitly in CLAUDE.md so it isn't accidentally "fixed" to match Forgotten &
  Found's style later.
- Consistent original characters (Pip the bunny) matter more for a kids' channel than
  for history content — young viewers and the parents selecting videos for them
  recognize and return to a known character faster than they evaluate a new topic each
  time, which is why character-consistency via Canva `imageReferences` is called out as
  a dedicated rule rather than a nice-to-have.
